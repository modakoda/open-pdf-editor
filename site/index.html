What sets it apart

Real edits, not overlays.

Plenty of PDF tools only paint new things on top of the page. Open PDF Editor changes the page itself, so what you delete is gone, what you retype is real text, and what you sign stays verifiable.

01

Edit PDF text that's already on the page

Double-click any line of text in the PDF and type over it, right where it is. No text boxes stacked on top, no retyping whole paragraphs.

  • Retype text in place, matching its font, size and color
  • Move, resize, rotate, recolor or delete text, images and vector graphics
  • Bring forward or send back — edits keep their place in the stacking order
  • Deleted content is removed from the file, not covered up
02

Redact PDFs for real: removed, not hidden

Mark what has to go and review it. When you save, everything under the marks is taken out of the file — then the area is filled with the color you choose.

  • Removes text, vector graphics, image pixels, comments, links and form fields
  • “Redact all” marks every match of a search in one go
  • Remaining characters keep their exact positions
  • Sanitize strips metadata, attachments, JavaScript and hidden layers
03

Sign PDFs by hand, or with a certificate

Draw or type a signature and drop it on the line. When it has to hold up, sign cryptographically with your own certificate.

  • Sign with a .p12 / .pfx certificate, RSA or ECDSA
  • Added as an incremental update, so earlier signatures stay valid
  • Opening a file checks its signatures and whether it changed after signing
  • Your certificate password is used once to unlock the key and never stored

And everything else

One app for the whole job.

From a quick signature to preparing a form for a hundred people.

Text recognition, offline

Make scanned pages searchable, copyable and editable. OCR runs on your machine, and scans are detected when you open them.

Fill and build forms

Fill every field type, or add text, checkbox, radio, dropdown, list and signature fields of your own. Flatten on save.

Organize pages

Drag to reorder, rotate, duplicate, insert blank pages, merge other PDFs, extract pages or split into several files.

Annotate

Text, images, shapes, arrows, freehand ink, highlights, whiteout and sticky notes — still editable after you reopen the file.

Text in many scripts

Latin, Greek and Cyrillic fonts built in. Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai and Devanagari use your system's fonts.

Password protection

Encrypt with AES-256, or remove the password from a file you can open. Existing security settings are kept on save.

Watermarks & page numbers

Stamp every page, number them, and edit document properties: title, author, dates and custom entries.

Undo anything

Every change can be undone. Unsaved work is protected when you close a window or quit.

Stays up to date

New versions download in the background and install when you quit. Prefer to update by hand? Turn it off.

Questions

Good to know.

Is it really free?

Yes. Open PDF Editor is open source under the MIT license: no account, no trial, no paid tier and nothing stamped on your files. You can use it at home or at work.

Is it a free alternative to Adobe Acrobat?

For the everyday jobs, yes: editing existing text, redacting, filling and building forms, OCR, signing and organizing pages. It's free, works offline and runs on Linux too, where Acrobat doesn't. It doesn't do everything Acrobat does — for example, it doesn't yet check signers against trusted certificate authorities or type right-to-left scripts.

Does it upload my documents anywhere?

No. Everything, including text recognition and signing, runs on your computer. The only network request the app makes is checking for updates, which you can turn off under Help › Check for Updates Automatically.

Is the redaction safe for sensitive documents?

On save, the text, graphics and image pixels under each mark are removed from the file, along with comments, links and form fields there. “Sanitize document” also removes metadata, attachments, JavaScript and hidden layers. Use Redact rather than Whiteout (which only covers content), and check the result before sharing.

Can it check signatures on files I receive?

Yes. Opening a signed PDF verifies each signature's integrity, signer, time and reason, and tells you whether the file changed after signing. It does not yet check certificates against trusted authorities, so a signer's identity is shown as not verified.

Which languages can I type in?

Latin, Greek and Cyrillic work out of the box with bundled fonts. Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai and Devanagari use the fonts installed on your system. Right-to-left scripts such as Arabic and Hebrew aren't supported yet. Text recognition includes English and Lithuanian.

Why does macOS or Windows warn me when I open it?

Builds that aren't signed with a paid developer certificate show a warning the first time you open them. On macOS, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and click Open Anyway; on Windows, choose More info › Run anyway.
